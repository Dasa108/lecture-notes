How it works

From a recording to a revision deck

Three of the four steps run entirely on your phone. Only the finished transcript text leaves it, and only when you ask for notes.

Capture

Record in the app with the screen off, or import an audio or video file. Attach slides or a PDF if you have them.

On your phone

Transcribe

Whisper turns speech into timestamped text in the background. Long lectures are split into chunks and survive restarts.

On your phone

Label speakers

Voices are told apart, so the lecturer and students' questions are labelled. Rename or merge them with a tap.

On your phone

Make notes

An AI model you choose writes a summary, key terms, flashcards and a quiz, each linked back to its moment in the audio.

Your own API key
Features

Everything you need to revise

Built for real lectures: hours long, several voices, slides on the projector, and a whole term of subjects.

A transcript you can fix

Every line has a timestamp; tap it to hear that moment. If Whisper mishears a term all lecture, fix every line at once.

Cards go into a review deck with spaced repetition (SM-2): hard cards come back soon, easy ones wait weeks. Or shuffle everything before an exam.

Uses your slides

Text is pulled from attached slides and PDFs, with on-device OCR for scanned pages, so the notes use the same terms as your slides.

Practice quiz

One question at a time, with the explanation and a link to hear that part of the lecture again. Your best score is kept.

Export, and never lose a term's work

Export notes to Markdown, PDF or an Anki deck. Back up every lecture, recording, flashcard schedule and setting to one file, then restore it on a new phone.

Built for your phone

It checks your phone before it starts

The first time you open Speech models, the app reads your phone's memory and storage and times a 30-second test clip. It then recommends the most accurate model that can still transcribe a one-hour lecture in about 20 minutes.

  • Models that won't fit are blocked, not just labelled. The limits come from measured peak memory with at least 6× headroom, so Android won't close the app halfway through a lecture.
  • Older phones are told plainly if they can't transcribe on-device, and can still make notes from slides and PDFs.
  • Heavy work pauses when the phone gets hot and picks up where it left off.

Installing

  1. Open the download on your phone and tap the file.
  2. If Android asks, allow your browser to install unknown apps. You can turn it off again afterwards.
  3. Tap Install. If Play Protect warns about an unfamiliar app, choose Install anyway.
  4. Open Settings → Speech models and let it test your phone and download the recommended model.
  5. For study notes, add a key under Settings → Notes AI. Groq's is free and needs no card.

Updates install over the top and keep your lectures, as long as they come from this page.

FAQ

Questions

Something else? Open an issue on GitHub.

Does it work offline?

Recording, transcription, speaker labels, slide text, flashcard review and quizzes all work offline. You need a connection to download a speech model once, and to generate notes, unless you run Ollama on a computer on the same Wi-Fi.

Do I have to pay for anything?

No. The app is free, and Groq, Cerebras and OpenRouter all have free tiers that are enough for regular lectures. The app paces itself to stay inside their per-minute limits. Claude is the paid option if you want the best notes.

What languages does it understand?

English. The speech models are Whisper's English-only versions, which are more accurate for English at the same size.

How long does a lecture take to process?

It depends on the phone and the model. The app measures your phone and shows an estimate for each model before you download it. On a recent mid-range phone, a one-hour lecture takes around 15 minutes with the recommended model.

It got a word wrong in every line. Can I fix it?

Yes. Use find and replace in the transcript, with whole-word and match-case options. You can also edit single lines and rename or merge speakers. Make the notes after fixing, and they'll use the corrected text.

What happens if I switch phones?

Use Settings → Backup & restore to save everything to one file, including recordings, transcripts, notes, flashcard progress and settings, then restore it on the new phone. API keys aren't included, so add them again.

Under the hood

How it's built

A native Android app in Kotlin. Speech recognition is whisper.cpp, compiled for ARM with the Android NDK and called over JNI. Long jobs run in WorkManager, so they survive the app being closed.
