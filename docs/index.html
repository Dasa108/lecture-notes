From a recording to a revision deck

Most of the work happens on the phone. Only the finished transcript text leaves it, and only when you ask for notes.

Capture

Record in the app (it keeps going with the screen off), or import or share an audio or video file. Attach the slides or a PDF if you have them.

On your phone

Transcribe

whisper.cpp turns speech into timestamped text in the background, with a progress notification. Long lectures are split into chunks and survive restarts.

On your phone

Label speakers

sherpa-onnx separates the voices, so the lecturer and students' questions are told apart. Rename or merge speakers with a tap.

On your phone

Make notes

An AI model of your choice writes a summary, key terms, an outline, flashcards and a quiz, all linked back to the moment in the audio.

Your own API key

Everything you need to revise

Built for real lectures: hours long, several voices, slides on the projector, and a term's worth of subjects.

Record or import

Record whole lectures, hours long, or bring in m4a, mp3, wav, ogg, opus, mp4, mkv, webm, mov and more. From video, only the audio is kept.

Searchable transcript

Every line has a timestamp. Tap one to hear it. Search across all lectures, and fix misheard words everywhere at once with find and replace.

Uses your slides

Text is pulled from attached slides and PDFs, with on-device OCR for scanned pages, so the notes match the terms on the slides.

Flashcards that space out

Cards go into a review deck with spaced repetition (SM-2), so you see hard cards often and easy ones rarely. Or shuffle everything before an exam.

Practice quiz

One question at a time, with the explanation and a link to hear that part of the lecture again. Your best score is kept.

Export and back up

Export notes to Markdown, PDF or an Anki deck. Back up every lecture, recording and setting to one file, and restore it on a new phone.

It checks your phone before it starts

On first visit to Speech models, the app reads your phone's memory and storage, then times a 30-second test clip. It recommends the most accurate model that still transcribes a one-hour lecture in about 20 minutes.

  • Models that won't fit are blocked, not just labelled. The limits come from measured peak memory, with at least 6× headroom, so a model isn't closed by Android halfway through a lecture.
  • Older phones are told plainly if they can't transcribe on-device, and can still make notes from slides and PDFs.
  • Heavy work pauses when the phone gets hot and picks up where it left off.

Your lectures stay yours

There's no account, no server and no tracking. The app talks only to the services you set up.

  • Audio never leaves the phone. Transcription and speaker labelling run on the device.
  • You bring the AI. Use a free Groq, Cerebras or OpenRouter key, a paid Claude key, or Ollama on your own computer over Wi-Fi.
  • Keys are encrypted with the Android Keystore and left out of backups.
  • You see the cost first. Each notes run shows an estimated token count before anything is sent.

Installing

  1. Open the download on your phone and tap the file.
  2. If Android asks, allow your browser to install unknown apps. You can turn this off again afterwards.
  3. Tap Install. Play Protect may warn about an unfamiliar app; choose Install anyway.
  4. Open Lecture Notes, go to Settings → Speech models, and let it test your phone and download the recommended model.
  5. For study notes, add an API key under Settings → Notes AI. Groq's is free and needs no card.

Updates install over the top and keep your lectures, as long as they come from this page.

Questions

Does it work offline?

Recording, transcription, speaker labels, slide text, flashcard review and quizzes all work offline. You need a connection to download a speech model once, and to generate notes, unless you run Ollama on a computer on the same Wi-Fi.

Do I have to pay for anything?

No. The app is free, and Groq, Cerebras and OpenRouter all have free tiers that are enough for regular lectures. The app paces itself to stay inside their per-minute limits. Claude is the paid option if you want the best notes.

What languages does it understand?

English. The speech models are Whisper's English-only versions, which are more accurate for English at the same size.

How long does a lecture take to process?

It depends on the phone and the model. The app measures your phone and shows an estimate for each model before you download it. On a recent mid-range phone, a one-hour lecture takes around 15 minutes with the recommended model.

It got a word wrong in every line. Can I fix it?

Yes. Use find and replace in the transcript, with whole-word and match-case options. You can also edit single lines, and rename or merge speakers. Make the notes after fixing, and they'll use the corrected text.

What happens if I switch phones?

Use Settings → Backup & restore to save everything to one file, including recordings, transcripts, notes, flashcard progress and settings. Restore it on the new phone. API keys aren't included, so add them again.

How it's built

A native Android app in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose. Speech recognition is whisper.cpp compiled for ARM with the Android NDK and called over JNI. Long jobs run in WorkManager so they survive the app closing.
